This is a textbook tenancy deposit claim suited to the small-claims track, where costs are generally not recoverable even if you win — so paying a solicitor would likely cost more than you'd gain. Close the one real gap first (authoritative scheme confirmation and a written request to the landlord), then you can bring a county court claim yourself. Realistically expect a modest court fee, a few months to a hearing, and a likely award of the £980.77 deposit back plus a penalty of one to three times that figure at the court's discretion. Escalate to a solicitor only if the landlord produces evidence the deposit was in fact protected, or the matter becomes contested on facts.

Act 1 — Your strongest case

Jamie paid a £980.77 deposit to a private landlord on 13 April 2024 but it appears in none of the three statutory schemes and he was never given the prescribed information about where it was held. On the documents — the bank statement proving payment, the three 'No deposit found' searches and the tenancy agreement's own blank Schedule 1 — this is a clear breach of the landlord's obligations under s.213 of the Housing Act 2004, entitling Jamie to return of the deposit and a penalty of between one and three times its value under s.214(4).

Legal basis — verified verbatim against the corpus; ungrounded citations stripped

  • Housing Act 2004, s.213(3) “Where a landlord receives a tenancy deposit in connection with an assured tenancy, the initial requirements of an authorised scheme must be complied with by the landlord in relation to the deposit within the period of 30 days beginning with the date on which it is received.”
  • Housing Act 2004, s.213(6) “The information required by subsection (5) must be given to the tenant and any relevant person— (a) in the prescribed form or in a form substantially to the same effect, and (b) within the period of 30 days beginning with the date on which the deposit is received by the landlord.”
  • Housing Act 2004, s.214(3) “The court must, as it thinks fit, either— (a) order the person who appears to the court to be holding the deposit to repay it to the applicant, or (b) order that person to pay the deposit into the designated account held by the scheme administrator under an authorised custodial scheme, within the period of 14 days beginning with the date of the making of the order.”
  • Housing Act 2004, s.214(4) “The court must order the landlord to pay to the applicant a sum of money not less than the amount of the deposit and not more than three times the amount of the deposit within the period of 14 days beginning with the date of the making of the order.”
  • Housing Act 2004, s.214(1A) “Subsection (1) also applies in a case where the tenancy has ended, and in such a case the reference in subsection (1) to the tenant is to a person who was a tenant under the tenancy.”
