# Case Reality Report — Case 07 — The council keeps fobbing me off about the damp

**Prospects:** 🟡 arguable — Notice and worsening/recurrence are exceptionally well documented and the Council's own report logs a Category 1 hazard, but liability turns on the contested cause (penetrating damp vs condensation), on which there is no independent expert evidence yet.

**Recommendation:** `escalate-to-solicitor` — This is a housing disrepair/fitness claim with a possible personal-injury element for a child, where an independent expert and protocol compliance are pivotal — not a simple small-claims matter. A specialist housing disrepair solicitor will usually take this on a conditional ('no win, no fee') basis and can recover costs from the Council, so out-of-pocket cost to Crystal should be low; realistically expect several months (the protocol allows the landlord 20 working days to respond to the Letter of Claim, plus time to instruct a single joint expert and for works/negotiation). Crystal can start now, for free, by lodging the formal complaint and gathering the documents above, but she should not sign anything further from the Council or contractors without advice and should get a solicitor to commission the damp survey and draft the Letter of Claim.

## Act 1 — Your strongest case

Crystal is a secure council tenant with overwhelming evidence that she repeatedly put the Council on notice of damp and black mould (15 emails over eight months, a phone log, photos and a GP letter) and that the problem worsened and recurred after the Council's own remedial works failed. The Council's own inspection report records a Category 1 damp-and-mould hazard and recommended a specialist damp inspection on recurrence that never happened, which supports breach of the landlord's repairing covenant (s.11) and the fitness-for-human-habitation covenant (s.9A), the latter being a continuing duty that bites where mould returns within a month.

## Act 2 — The Steelman (how the other side comes at you)

- **The Council will say the damp is lifestyle condensation, not a structural defect the landlord must remedy, relying on its inspector's diagnosis and Crystal's drying of clothes indoors. If the unfitness is mainly attributable to the tenant's own conduct, the s.11/s.9A duty is not engaged.**
  - ✅ grounded: “The cause is provisionally assessed as condensation due to insufficient ventilation rather than ingress of water. The combination of elevated indoor relative humidity, restricted bathroom extraction, and the tenant’s reported drying of clothing indoors is consistent with this diagnosis. No evidence of penetrating damp was identified during this inspection.”
  - ⚖️ Landlord and Tenant Act 1985, s.9A(3): “The implied covenant is also not to be taken as imposing on the lessor any liability in respect of the dwelling being unfit for human habitation if the unfitness is wholly or mainly attributable to—

(a) the lessee's own breach of covenant”
  - ⚖️ Landlord and Tenant Act 1985, s.11(2)(a): “to carry out works or repairs for which the lessee is liable by virtue of his duty to use the premises in a tenant-like manner”
- **The Council will point to the works-completion form Crystal signed, arguing she confirmed the works were done to her satisfaction and waived further claims about them. (This is weakened by the form's own statutory-obligations saving and by s.9A(4), which voids any term excluding the fitness covenant — but the opponent will still run it.)**
  - ✅ grounded: “I confirm that the above works have been carried out to my satisfaction.
By signing this declaration the tenant acknowledges completion of the works listed and waives any further claim arising from the items specified in this works order.”
  - ⚖️ Landlord and Tenant Act 1985, s.9A(4): “Any provision of a lease or of any agreement relating to a lease (whether made before or after the grant or creation of the lease) is void to the extent that it purports—

(a) to exclude or limit the obligations of the lessor under the implied covenant”
- **The Council will argue there is no independent expert evidence of a structural cause: its own external check found no defect, so Crystal's belief that it is penetrating damp is unproven. The burden is on her to show the disrepair flows from a structural cause within s.11.**
  - ✅ grounded: “External street-level inspection of the south elevation brickwork did not identify any obvious defect. Rainwater goods on the south elevation appear clear from ground observation. No access was obtained to roof level on this visit.”
- **The Council can say Crystal has not yet complied with the pre-action steps a court expects before issuing — no formal complaint and no Letter of Claim — which can have costs and case-management consequences.**
  - ✅ grounded: “I haven't made a formal complaint or sent any kind of legal letter. A friend mentioned something about a 'pre-action protocol' but I don't know what that means.”
  - ⚖️ Pre-Action Protocol for Housing Conditions Claims (England), para 5.2: “The tenant must send a Letter of Claim containing:

(a) the tenant's name, the address of the property, the tenant's address if different, the tenant's telephone number and when access is available; (b) details of the defects, including any defects outstanding, in the form of a schedule, if appropriate; (c) history of the defects, including any attempts to rectify them; (d) details of any notification previously given to the landlord”

## Act 3 — Evidence gaps to close (your checklist)

- [ ] Obtain an independent damp survey (ideally via the protocol's single-joint-expert route) to establish whether the cause is penetrating/structural damp or condensation — this is the single most important piece of evidence and directly answers the Council's condensation defence.
- [ ] Send a protocol-compliant Letter of Claim setting out the schedule of defects, full history, all prior notifications (the 15 emails and phone log), the effect on the family, special damages and the proposed expert.
- [ ] Make the formal two-stage complaint (Stage 1/Stage 2) and keep the Housing Ombudsman route open as recorded in the tenancy agreement.
- [ ] Compile an itemised inventory and photographs of damaged belongings to support a special-damages claim.
- [ ] Get the original signed works form reviewed and preserve the contractor wording showing the works were 'Routine R3' with redecoration excluded and the saving that it 'does not affect the Council's ongoing statutory obligations'.
- [ ] Ask the GP for a fuller report or, if pursued as personal injury, a medico-legal report linking Daniel's respiratory symptoms to the conditions.
- [ ] Request the Council's post-works 28-day and 90-day inspection records (recommended in its own report) to show they were never carried out.

## Legal basis (verified against the corpus — ungrounded citations stripped)

- **Landlord and Tenant Act 1985, s.11(1)(a)** — “to keep in repair the structure and exterior of the dwelling-house (including drains, gutters and external pipes)”
- **Landlord and Tenant Act 1985, s.9A(1)** — “there is implied a covenant by the lessor that the dwelling—

(a) is fit for human habitation at the time the lease is granted or otherwise created or, if later, at the beginning of the term of the lease, and

(b) will remain fit for human habitation during the term of the lease.”
- **Landlord and Tenant Act 1985, s.9A(5)** — “Where in any proceedings before a court it is alleged that a lessor is in breach of an obligation under the implied covenant, the court may order specific performance of the obligation (regardless of any equitable rule restricting the scope of that remedy).”
- **Pre-Action Protocol for Housing Conditions Claims (England), para 6.2** — “The landlord should normally reply to the Letter of Claim within 20 working days of receipt.”
- **Pre-Action Protocol for Housing Conditions Claims (England), para 7.2(a)** — “If the landlord does not raise an objection to the proposed expert or letter of instruction within 20 working days of receipt of the Letter of Claim, the expert should be instructed as a single joint expert, using the tenant's proposed letter of instruction.”
