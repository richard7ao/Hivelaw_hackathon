# Case Reality Report — Case 01 — My landlord never protected my deposit

**Prospects:** 🟢 strong — The documentary record — proof of payment, three nil searches, and the landlord's own incomplete Schedule 1 — points squarely to non-protection and non-provision of prescribed information, both of which trigger the mandatory penalty under s.214(4).

**Recommendation:** `self-serve` — This is a textbook tenancy deposit claim suited to the small-claims track, where costs are generally not recoverable even if you win — so paying a solicitor would likely cost more than you'd gain. Close the one real gap first (authoritative scheme confirmation and a written request to the landlord), then you can bring a county court claim yourself. Realistically expect a modest court fee, a few months to a hearing, and a likely award of the £980.77 deposit back plus a penalty of one to three times that figure at the court's discretion. Escalate to a solicitor only if the landlord produces evidence the deposit was in fact protected, or the matter becomes contested on facts.

**Documents ingested:** 2 PDF(s) (bank_statement_deposit_payment.pdf, tenancy_agreement.pdf); 3 image(s) noted but not ingested (v1 is text-only)

## Act 1 — Your strongest case

Jamie paid a £980.77 deposit to a private landlord on 13 April 2024 but it appears in none of the three statutory schemes and he was never given the prescribed information about where it was held. On the documents — the bank statement proving payment, the three 'No deposit found' searches and the tenancy agreement's own blank Schedule 1 — this is a clear breach of the landlord's obligations under s.213 of the Housing Act 2004, entitling Jamie to return of the deposit and a penalty of between one and three times its value under s.214(4).

## Act 2 — The Steelman (how the other side comes at you)

- **The three screenshots are tenant-run self-service searches, which are not conclusive proof that the deposit was never protected — some scheme search tools only match on exact reference/postcode details, and insured-scheme deposits in particular may not show up. The landlord can demand authoritative confirmation that no deposit is held before liability is accepted.**
  - ✅ grounded: “I've checked all three schemes, DPS, MyDeposits and TDS, and my deposit isn't in any of them.”
  - ⚖️ Housing Act 2004, s.214(1)(b): “that he has been notified by the landlord that a particular authorised scheme applies to the deposit but has been unable to obtain confirmation from the scheme administrator that the deposit is being held in accordance with the scheme.”
- **The landlord can argue he substantially complied by setting out almost all the prescribed information in Schedule 1 of the signed agreement at the outset, with only the scheme name to follow on registration — and that the agreement expressly committed him to protecting the deposit within 30 days.**
  - ✅ grounded: “The Landlord shall, within thirty (30) days of receipt of the Deposit, protect it in an Authorised Scheme in accordance with section 213 of the Housing Act 2004.”
  - ⚖️ Housing Act 2004, s.213(6): “The information required by subsection (5) must be given to the tenant and any relevant person—
(a) in the prescribed form or in a form substantially to the same effect, and
(b) within the period of 30 days beginning with the date on which the deposit is received by the landlord.”
- **Even if liability is established, the landlord will argue for the minimum 1× penalty (not 3×), pointing to his otherwise good compliance record — gas safety, EICR, smoke/CO alarms and How to Rent guide all in order — to characterise this as an innocent oversight rather than a deliberate breach.**
  - ❌ UNVERIFIED — quote not found verbatim in the file: “Gas Safety Certificate | Last issued 07 February 2024 by Bromsgrove Gas Services Ltd (Gas Safe Reg. 558112). A copy has been supplied to the Tenant.”
  - ⚖️ Housing Act 2004, s.214(4): “The court must order the landlord to pay to the applicant a sum of money not less than the amount of the deposit and not more than three times the amount of the deposit within the period of 14 days beginning with the date of the making of the order.”

## Act 3 — Evidence gaps to close (your checklist)

- [ ] Request formal written confirmation from each of DPS, MyDeposits and TDS that no deposit is registered for the property/tenant — a letter or email from the scheme administrator is far stronger evidence than a self-service screenshot.
- [ ] Send the landlord a dated written request (email/letter) asking which scheme the deposit is protected in and for a copy of the prescribed information, so his failure to confirm is documented before issuing.
- [ ] Preserve and print the bank statement proving the £980.77 payment to 'P SULLIVAN' on 13 April 2024 (already in the file) as the proof the deposit was paid and received.
- [ ] Get the landlord's 'cagey' refusals into writing (follow up any phone calls with a confirming email) so the court sees the deposit was withheld and the breach unresolved.
- [ ] Confirm and record the current tenancy status (still running vs ended), as this affects which limb of s.214 applies and whether a separate deposit-return order is needed.

## Legal basis (verified against the corpus — ungrounded citations stripped)

- **Housing Act 2004, s.213(3)** — “Where a landlord receives a tenancy deposit in connection with an assured tenancy, the initial requirements of an authorised scheme must be complied with by the landlord in relation to the deposit within the period of 30 days beginning with the date on which it is received.”
- **Housing Act 2004, s.213(6)** — “The information required by subsection (5) must be given to the tenant and any relevant person—
(a) in the prescribed form or in a form substantially to the same effect, and
(b) within the period of 30 days beginning with the date on which the deposit is received by the landlord.”
- **Housing Act 2004, s.214(3)** — “The court must, as it thinks fit, either—
(a) order the person who appears to the court to be holding the deposit to repay it to the applicant, or
(b) order that person to pay the deposit into the designated account held by the scheme administrator under an authorised custodial scheme,
within the period of 14 days beginning with the date of the making of the order.”
- **Housing Act 2004, s.214(4)** — “The court must order the landlord to pay to the applicant a sum of money not less than the amount of the deposit and not more than three times the amount of the deposit within the period of 14 days beginning with the date of the making of the order.”
- **Housing Act 2004, s.214(1A)** — “Subsection (1) also applies in a case where the tenancy has ended, and in such a case the reference in subsection (1) to the tenant is to a person who was a tenant under the tenancy.”
