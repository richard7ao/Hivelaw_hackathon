# Case 10 — I took my neighbour to court and lost, can I try again?

**Small Claims Appeal Case 10** | Lawhive Hackathon 2026 | Case Brief / Messy, Severe

**Claimant:** George Bertram Dobbs, 63, retired teacher, Sheffield

---

## Problem, in the client's own words

My neighbour has a large oak tree near the boundary of our properties. Over the past few years the roots have caused serious damage to my garden wall and patio. I got a quote for £4,200 to put it all right and I wanted my neighbour to pay it.

I tried talking to them first but they refused, saying the tree was there when I bought the house and it's not their problem. So I took them to small claims court myself. I thought the facts were straightforward and I could handle it.

I got it wrong. I didn't get a surveyor's report, I just used photos and a general assessment from the builder who gave me the quote. I also didn't follow the court's directions properly. I missed the deadline to file my witness statement and sent it in late. I didn't file a proper schedule of loss either, just attached the quote.

At the hearing, my neighbour had a solicitor. They argued I hadn't proven the tree actually caused the damage, they said it could have been pre-existing subsidence. The judge dismissed my claim. He specifically mentioned the lack of expert evidence and my failure to comply with the directions.

That was six weeks ago. I'm still certain the tree caused the damage, I've lived here for nearly forty years and the problems started when the tree got big, but I accept I didn't present the case properly.

Is there anything I can do? Can I appeal? Can I start again? Or have I lost my chance? I should say the £4,200 is a lot of money for me as a retiree and I'm not sure how much more I can afford to spend pursuing this further.
