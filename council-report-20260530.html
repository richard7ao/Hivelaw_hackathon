Where the Council Agrees

1. The pitch concept is the strongest asset. "Every legal AI tells you you're right. We built the one that tells you how you'll lose" passes the ten-second test. The three-act structure is clean, the access-to-justice framing with sourced statistics is rigorous, and the Crystal case is well-chosen.

2. The anti-hallucination architecture is real. Two-tier guard: soft-fail for steelman quotes (flag but keep), hard-fail for legal citations (strip entirely if not in corpus). This is genuine engineering, not vaporware.

3. Demo-readiness is the weakest dimension. Every advisor flags seams: dead-end dashboard cases, hardcoded floating chat, window.print() PDF, no "reconsider-pursuing" case to prove the honest "no."

Where the Council Clashes

The mock data question. The Contrarian calls the pre-cached reports a "structural lie" (61/100). The First Principles Thinker and Expansionist see it as a sound design decision for demo reliability (82-84/100). The Chairman sides with the latter — PITCH.md explicitly documents this as deliberate, and pre-cached responses from real Claude runs are not fake data.

Technical depth. Scores range 52 to 85. The Contrarian under-credits the actual engineering (PDF ingestion, structured output, prompt caching, local fallback engine). The Executor correctly identifies this as substantial for a one-day build.

Blind Spots the Council Caught

  • Dead-end dashboard cases (Outsider): 2 of 4 cards go nowhere. A judge who clicks wrong hits a dead end.
  • No "reconsider-pursuing" case (Executor): The pitch's central claim ("the honest no") has no demo proof.
  • Floating chat is hardcoded (Contrarian): Canned responses in the report page chatbot vs. live API in the intake chat — a curious judge will find the seam.
  • Nobody caught: The intake prompt engineering is sophisticated — progressive report building, readiness scoring across 8 buckets, 2-turn convergence ceiling. The corpus files are real verbatim statute text. These are under-marketed strengths.

The Recommendation

This is a top-2 project at a hackathon of this caliber. The concept is genuinely differentiated — no other team in the room will have an agent that argues against the user using their own documents, verifiably. The risk profile is asymmetric in the team's favor: the 3-minute recorded demo path is solid, and vulnerabilities only surface in post-pitch exploration.

The project's ceiling is high 80s. The gap between 80 and 88+ is entirely demo polish — all fixable in under an hour.

The One Thing to Do First

Remove the two dead-end dashboard cases (heating failure, noise complaint). A dashboard with two fully-functioning cases is more impressive than four where half are broken. This is a 5-minute change and eliminates the highest-probability post-pitch failure mode.
