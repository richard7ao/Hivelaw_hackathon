## 11 Repairing obligations in short leases

(1) In a lease to which this section applies there is implied a covenant by the lessor—

(a) to keep in repair the structure and exterior of the dwelling-house (including drains, gutters and external pipes),

(b) to keep in repair and proper working order the installations in the dwelling-house for the supply of water, gas and electricity and for sanitation (including basins, sinks, baths and sanitary conveniences, but not other fixtures, fittings and appliances for making use of the supply of water, gas or electricity), and

(c) to keep in repair and proper working order the installations in the dwelling-house for space heating and heating water.

(2) The covenant implied by subsection (1) ("the lessor's repairing covenant") shall not be construed as requiring the lessor—

(a) to carry out works or repairs for which the lessee is liable by virtue of his duty to use the premises in a tenant-like manner, or would be so liable but for an express covenant on his part,

(b) to rebuild or reinstate the premises in the case of destruction or damage by fire, or by tempest, flood or other inevitable accident, or

(c) to keep in repair or maintain anything which the lessee is entitled to remove from the dwelling-house.

(3) In determining the standard of repair required by the lessor's repairing covenant, regard shall be had to the age, character and prospective life of the dwelling-house and the locality in which it is situated.

(6) In a lease in which the lessor's repairing covenant is implied there is also implied a covenant by the lessee that the lessor, or any person authorised by him in writing, may at reasonable times of the day and on giving 24 hours' notice in writing to the occupier, enter the premises comprised in the lease for the purpose of viewing their condition and state of repair.

## How this applies to a damp/mould disrepair claim (evidence & procedure, not advice)

- s.11 puts the duty to keep the **structure and exterior** in repair on the landlord. Penetrating damp (water entering through the structure) falls within this; pure lifestyle **condensation** generally does not — which is exactly why the *cause* is the battleground.
- The burden is on the tenant to show (a) the landlord was **on notice** of the disrepair, and (b) the disrepair flows from a **structural** cause within s.11, not tenant lifestyle.
- This is why the **independent damp survey** is the single most important evidence gap: it converts "I say it's the building / they say it's condensation" into an expert finding.

## Typical outcome range (general practice, NOT named case law)

- Disrepair claims commonly seek **general damages** for the discomfort/inconvenience of living with the disrepair (often assessed by reference to the period affected and the rent), plus **special damages** for damaged belongings.
- Do not state a specific percentage or figure as guaranteed — this is fact-specific and at the court's discretion. Flag any number for lawyer verification.
