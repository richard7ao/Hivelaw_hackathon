## Aims of the protocol (para 2.1)

The protocol's objectives are:

(a) avoid unnecessary litigation; (b) promote the speedy and appropriate carrying out of any remedial works which are the landlord's responsibility; (c) ensure that tenants receive any compensation to which they are entitled as speedily as possible; (d) promote good pre-litigation practice, including the early exchange of information; (e) give guidance about the instruction of experts; and (f) keep the costs of resolving disputes down.

## The tenant's Letter of Claim (para 5.2)

The tenant must send a Letter of Claim containing:

(a) the tenant's name, the address of the property, the tenant's address if different, the tenant's telephone number and when access is available; (b) details of the defects, including any defects outstanding, in the form of a schedule, if appropriate; (c) history of the defects, including any attempts to rectify them; (d) details of any notification previously given to the landlord; (e) the effect of the defects on the tenant (including any personal injury claim); (f) the identities of all other persons who plan to make a personal injury claim and brief details of their personal injury claims; (g) the details of any special damages; (h) the proposed expert; (i) the proposed letter of instruction to the expert; and (j) relevant documents disclosed by the tenant.

## Landlord's response time (para 6.2)

The landlord should normally reply to the Letter of Claim within 20 working days of receipt.

## Single joint expert (para 7.2(a))

If the landlord does not raise an objection to the proposed expert or letter of instruction within 20 working days of receipt of the Letter of Claim, the expert should be instructed as a single joint expert, using the tenant's proposed letter of instruction.

## Why this matters to the verdict (evidence & procedure, not advice)

- A judge expects the tenant to have followed this protocol *before* issuing.
  Skipping it, or sending a non-compliant Letter of Claim, can have costs
  consequences later — which is exactly the kind of procedural trap the Steelman
  surfaces in the evidence-gap checklist.
- The Letter of Claim list (para 5.2) is effectively a ready-made checklist of what
  Crystal needs to assemble: a schedule of defects, the history, prior notifications
  (her 15 emails), the effect on her (the GP letter), special damages (damaged
  belongings), and the proposed expert (the independent damp survey).
- The 20-working-day response window and single-joint-expert mechanism set realistic
  expectations on *time* — feeding the honest cost/time read in the recommendation.
