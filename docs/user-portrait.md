## The category line (open and close on this)

> Every other legal AI answers one question: **"Are you right?"** — and it tells
> you yes. We answer the only question that actually protects you: **"How will you
> lose?"**

This is a *category* claim, not a feature claim. Don't lead with "free, fast,
honest" — cheaper-and-faster is what every tool says. Lead with the question only
we ask.

## The two users

The before/after lands on **two** people, not one. Miss the second and you drop
the angle Lawhive cares about commercially.

### User 1 — The litigant-in-person (our primary user)

**Who:** A person with a consumer legal dispute and no lawyer. From our 10 cases:
Jamie (deposit never protected), Crystal (council damp, single mother), plus
faulty-goods, withheld-wages, builder-dispute, unfair-dismissal claimants. Not
poor-poor — often working people for whom £200 is real money and a solicitor is a
last resort, not a first call.

**Their job (the one they're trying to do):** *"Do I actually have a case, and is
it worth the fight?"* They are not trying to "consume legal services." They are
trying to make one decision: pursue, settle, or walk away — without getting
crushed.

**Their day with the problem:**
- It's emotional and it's late. They're angry, anxious, and certain they're right.
- They Google it at 11pm and read ten contradictory forum threads.
- They ask ChatGPT, which validates them ("you have a strong claim!").
- Maybe they call Citizens Advice and get general advice, but no one reads *their*
  evidence and tells them where it's weak.
- They carry "a wildly distorted picture of what pursuing a claim actually looks
  like" (Lawhive's own words) — they underestimate cost, time, and how much
  evidence a judge needs.

**What they don't know (the gap we close):** that their own documents contain the
weapon that beats them. Crystal doesn't know the satisfaction form she signed is
the council's best defence. Jamie doesn't know screenshots aren't proof of
non-protection.

**The emotional "before" (the access-to-justice stake):** Crystal isn't a
"litigant-in-person." She's a single mother whose youngest has a cough from black
mould, who's been fobbed off for eight months, and who has *already* signed one
form that could sink her case without realising it. That's the real status quo:
vulnerable people grind doomed claims into the ground, or abandon winnable ones
out of fear and confusion — and either way they find out the expensive way, in
month eleven. The system makes them learn the hard truth too late, alone, after
the money's gone. That's the gap. Not efficiency — dignity.

### User 2 — The Lawhive solicitor (our GTM user)

**Who:** A consumer-legal solicitor on Lawhive's platform receiving intake.

**Their job:** Triage enquiries fast — separate "real case, worth my time" from
"no case" from "needs more before I can assess" — without doing a deep unpaid
review on every one.

**Their day (the pain, from the problem pack):** Intake arrives "basic and vague."
They can't assess merit at first contact. They do a second, deeper review once
they've chased the client for detail. Dead-end enquiries cost them time they don't
bill. The gap between "client describing their situation" and "lawyer working a
case" is unstructured and expensive.

**What they want:** a prepared case file — case type identified, key facts and
dates surfaced, evidence gaps already named, a realistic merits read. Exactly what
the Steelman's Case Reality Report *is*.

## The status quo: how case evaluation happens today (the "before")

### For the consumer — four options, all broken

| Today's option | What they get | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **Google / forums** | Ten conflicting opinions | No one reads *their* evidence; false confidence |
| **ChatGPT / generic AI** | "You have a strong claim!" | Validates them; never shows how they lose |
| **Citizens Advice** | Good general advice | Advice, not a case-specific evidence read; stretched thin |
| **Pay a solicitor** | A real assessment | £150–300 for an initial consult they often can't afford or won't risk on a maybe |

*The common failure: nothing tells them how they'll lose before they've sunk
months and money in.*

### For the solicitor

Unstructured, low-information intake → can't assess merit early → second deep
review → dead-end enquiries burn unbilled time.

## Before / After

### Consumer

| | BEFORE (status quo) | AFTER (The Steelman) |
|---|---|---|
| Question answered | "Am I right?" (and everyone says yes) | "How will I lose, and is it worth it?" |

| What they leave with | Confidence, often misplaced | A prospects read + an evidence-gap checklist they can act on |

| | BEFORE | AFTER |

*This is why the honest "no" is a feature Lawhive wants, not lead cannibalisation:

## The Q&A grenade (pre-load this answer)

A sharp judge will ask: *"Aren't you just discouraging people from seeking justice?
You've built a tool that talks people out of suing."* If this fumbles, the whole
premise wobbles. Answer, crisp and rehearsed:

> "The opposite. We tell people with *real* cases to fight — prepared, with their
> gaps already closed. And we save people with *hopeless* cases from a year of
> misery and a costs order they never saw coming. Knowing you'll lose **is** access
> to justice. Today's system lets people find that out the expensive way, in month
> eleven, after the money's gone. We move that truth to day one, for free."

Turn the hardest question into the strongest moment: the honest "no" is *more*
access to justice, not less. (This is also the answer to "isn't this bad for
Lawhive's lead volume?" — filtering out the non-cases lowers cost-to-serve and
raises the quality of every enquiry that lands.)

## Claims to sanity-check before stage (no lawyer — engineer self-review)

We have no legal professional to validate these, so the rule is: only assert what's
verifiable or sourced, and soften anything we can't stand behind in a room of real
lawyers. A Lawhive lawyer pressure-testing these is the explicit v2 loop.

- **Initial-consult fee range (£150–300)** — illustrative. Either find a citable
  source or soften to "a few hundred pounds" (which `PITCH.md` already does). Don't
  let a soft number anchor the "before" and become the thing a judge challenges.
- **Citizens Advice "general advice, not case-specific evidence read"** — keep it
  factual and non-dismissive (they're respected; one is even a GTM partner). Frame
  as a gap in the *system*, not a knock on them.
- **Solicitor-intake pain points** — quoted/paraphrased from the Lawhive problem
  pack. Keep close to the source so the room recognises their own words.
