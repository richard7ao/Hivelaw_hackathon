# Design: The Steelman — the legal agent that argues against you

## Problem Statement

Lawhive Hackathon, 30 May 2026, Track B / Track A. The team scrapped its prior
plan (a damp-and-mould claim-accelerator) after an LLM council found it answered
the wrong question: it always says "yes, here's your letter," but Lawhive's own
lawyers said the most valuable thing an agent can do is sometimes talk someone
out of a claim. The brief's deepest stated insight: *"if the most useful thing
an agent could do is talk someone out of pursuing the wrong claim, what does
success look like?"*

Almost every consumer-legal AI validates the user. None of them show the user
how they will lose. That gap — between what you believe happened and what a
judge will need to see — is the single biggest source of friction at the start
of any matter (the brief's words).

## What Makes This Cool

The agent **role-plays the opponent's best case against you**, grounded in your
own documents, so you feel the strongest punch before a judge throws it. It is
recalibration made *visceral* instead of preachy. The hero moment is the agent
quoting the user's own evidence back at them as a weapon — e.g. in the provided
Case 07, the agent says: *"The council will point to the form you signed that
says 'I confirm the works have been carried out to my satisfaction.' Here's how
we close that gap."*

It turns the panel's hardest insight into a memorable live moment, and it is
**impossible to fake**: a judge picks a case folder, the agent argues against
them in real time on documents nobody rehearsed.

## Constraints

- ~18 hours to build, demo, and rehearse. 3 people (thinker, engineer, lawyer).
- Working prototype a judge can interact with > polish (their stated rubric).
- 10 synthetic cases already in `/cases` as markdown (ingestion is trivial).
- No 3D, no PDF, no multi-agent, no agentic tool loop. v1 is a **single prompt,
  single completion, structured output**. The cases are 2–6 KB — the whole case
  fits in context, so no retrieval tools are needed. Cut anything off the
  critical path.
- **v1 ingests text only.** Image evidence (Case 07's mould photos, Case 01's
  scheme-search screenshots) is referenced from the case's document manifest but
  not analysed by vision. Acceptable because both hero beats are text-grounded
  (the signed satisfaction form; the tenancy/bank docs). Do not rabbit-hole on
  vision.

## Premises (all confirmed with the team)

1. **The Steelman is the middle act, not the whole show.** Three beats:
   (1) "Here's your strongest case" — validate first; (2) "Here's how the other
   side comes at you" — the steelman; (3) "Here's where you actually stand and
   what to do" — the verdict. Framed as a shield: *better to hear their best
   punch from me than from a judge.* — AGREED (three-act shield).
2. **Every counter-argument must be grounded in the case documents.** Grounded =
   substance; generic = gimmick. The agent must quote the user's own files.
   — AGREED.
3. **Interactivity is the build, not a feature.** The win condition is "a judge
   picks any case folder and it runs live." Robust ingestion of arbitrary
   provided documents is the spine, built first. — AGREED.
4. **There must be a tangible artifact: the Case Reality Report.** Your position
   → their best attack → your evidence gaps (an actionable checklist) → a
   prospects read → recommended next step (close the gap yourself, or escalate
   to a Lawhive solicitor *with the file*). The checklist is both the
   access-to-justice payload and the GTM handoff. — AGREED.
5. **Stay all-Anthropic, ignore the GCP/Gemini nod.** Anthropic is on the panel;
   the night before is not the time to add Vertex/Gemini plumbing. Accept the
   small ding, keep pure focus on the core agent. — AGREED (team decision).

Note on legal-correctness risk: steelmanning lives mostly in **evidence and
procedure** (burden of proof, what a judge needs to see, limitation periods,
pre-action conduct). That is domain-general and far safer than citing a 29-day-old
statute — it sidesteps the single-point-of-failure that sank the prior plan.
Every legal assertion still gets lawyer red-pen (see Assignment).

## Approaches Considered (idea-level alternatives)

- **Approach A — The Honest Mirror (Track B):** agent that tells the truth incl.
  "you don't have a case." Exactly the panel's question, but crowded track,
  weaker artifact, the "no" is hard to make crisp.
- **Approach B — Case in a Box (Track C):** adaptive intake → clean verified case
  brief. Strong "six months" story, but it's the thing Lawhive says it is
  *already building* — pitching into their roadmap.
- **Approach C — Protocol Guard (Track D):** catches non-compliant Letters Before
  Action. Concrete and verifiable, but dry to demo.
- **Approach D — The Steelman (lateral, CHOSEN):** argues the opponent's best
  case against you. Most memorable, impossible to fake, recalibration made
  visceral, with the Honest-Mirror verdict baked in as Act 3.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach D, The Steelman**, with the Honest Mirror's verdict as its third act.
It is the only option that converts the panel's own hardest insight into a
*memorable* interactive moment rather than a quiet one, and the access-to-justice
line writes itself: *"every other tool tells you you're right; the one that tells
you how you'll lose is the one that actually protects you."*

## The demo: 3-minute slot, 3 speakers (HARD constraint)

Three minutes with all three teammates speaking means **one pre-loaded case, one
hero beat, ~1 minute each**. No judge-picks-a-case here (no time). The
interactivity sacrifice is bought back by credibility: the team's own **lawyer
validates the steelman live** — the one thing no other team can fake. Everything
renders from pre-cached real responses so it's instant and deterministic.

Featured case: **Case 07 (Crystal, damp)** — the signed "works carried out to my
satisfaction" form is the killer steelman quote.

## The v0 three-act prompt (KEYSTONE — write this first, before anything else)

The lawyer cannot validate a prompt that doesn't exist, and the engineer and
lawyer must test the *same* artifact. So this gets written before the hand-run
and before any UI code. Draft v0:

```
You are an honest case-assessment assistant for a person in England with a
consumer legal dispute. You are NOT their solicitor. Your job is to show them
the truth about their case — including how the other side will attack it —
before they spend time and money they can't get back.

You will be given the full text of a case file (the client's account plus their
documents). Work ONLY from what is in the file. Never invent facts, statutes, or
quotes.

Produce three acts, then a verdict, as JSON:

1. best_case: the client's single strongest position, in plain English.
2. opponent_steelman: an array of the 2–4 strongest arguments the OTHER SIDE will
   make. Each item: { "argument", "source_quote" }. source_quote MUST be text
   that appears verbatim in the case file (quote the client's own document that
   the opponent will weaponise). If you cannot ground an argument in the file,
   do not include it.
3. evidence_gaps: an array of concrete, actionable things the client should get
   or do to close each weakness (e.g. "obtain an independent damp survey").
4. prospects: one of "strong" | "arguable" | "weak", plus one sentence of why.
5. recommendation: one of "self-serve" | "escalate-to-solicitor" |
   "reconsider-pursuing", plus the realistic cost/time/effort the client faces.

Ground every legal point in evidence and procedure (burden of proof, what a
judge needs to see, limitation periods, pre-action conduct), not in citing
specific statute unless the file makes it unambiguous. When unsure, say so.
```

**Anti-hallucination guard (engineer, ~30 min, non-negotiable):** after the model
returns, string-match every `source_quote` against the ingested case text. Strip
or flag any quote that does not literally appear. The entire credibility claim is
"it quotes your own evidence" — a fabricated quote inverts the demo live.

## Scope addition — legal grounding via a statute corpus (CEO review, 29 May)

The first 10-case run came out strong but **legally thin** — it says "the
deposit-protection rules" where a lawyer wants "Housing Act 2004, s.214" and "the
1–3× penalty." Decision: add real legal grounding, but make it safe the same way
the steelman is safe — a closed corpus we control, never free recall. This holds
the "safer than statute" premise (a wrong statute in front of housing solicitors
is the v1 trap); it does not abandon it.

**Accepted (build for tomorrow, ~3–4h):**
- `/corpus/` — real statute text pulled verbatim from legislation.gov.uk (no
  paraphrase) for the two demo domains: Housing Act 2004 ss.213–215 (deposit +
  s.214(4) 1–3× penalty), Landlord and Tenant Act 1985 s.11 + Homes (Fitness for
  Human Habitation) Act 2018, Pre-Action Protocol for Housing Conditions Claims,
  plus a short per-domain "typical outcome ranges" note.
- Prompt + schema: inject the corpus (prompt-cached — now large enough to actually
  cache); add `legal_basis: [{ citation, source_quote }]` to steelman points and
  the verdict. Cite statute ONLY from the corpus; if nothing applies, say so.
- **Extend the verification guard, legal quotes are a HARD fail.** Steelman quote
  fails → flag ⚠. Legal citation quote not found in the corpus → **strip it
  entirely.** A wrong statute is worse than no statute.

**Deferred (v2 / "six months"):** named case-law precedent. LLMs hallucinate case
citations (lawyers have been sanctioned for it); for tomorrow we use corpus-backed
*outcome ranges*, not named cases. A verified case corpus is the v2 upgrade.

**Known degradation:** the corpus covers housing only. The runner processes all 10
cases; non-housing ones (employment, consumer) get thin/no legal grounding and must
say "no corpus provision applies" rather than inventing one — the hard-fail guard
enforces this. Demo only features housing cases, so this is fine.

## The 18-hour build plan

**Engineer (critical path, in order):**
1. Next.js + chat UI scaffold (reuse a template, move fast).
2. **Document ingestion FIRST.** Accept any of the provided case files
   (markdown today; PDFs via simple text extraction), parse to text, feed to
   Claude. Must be robust to arbitrary docs, not hardcoded to one case. This IS
   criterion 2.
3. The three-act agent: the v0 prompt above, single completion, structured JSON
   output. No tool loop. Add the `source_quote` string-match guard.
4. Case Reality Report view: render the JSON cleanly. The evidence-gap checklist
   is the hero artifact. HTML page on localhost — PDF is out of scope.
5. Live-pick flow: dropdown of **only the lawyer-validated cases** (not all 10 if
   some fail the hand-test) + an optional file-upload fallback that runs but
   carries no soundness guarantee. Rehearse the dropdown path.
6. **Pre-cache the JSON for every validated case.** Render from cache live (the
   responses are real, just pre-generated), with the live API as the "want me to
   run it fresh?" backup. Removes venue-network / latency / rate-limit risk from
   the highest-attention moment without faking anything.

**Lawyer (tonight — this is the spec):**
- Hand-run all 10 cases through Claude using the v0 prompt above.
- **Output a sign-off table per case** committed to the repo
  (`case_NN | steelman point | sound? Y/N | corrected wording`). The engineer
  hardens prompts from this; it's what makes "every assertion gets lawyer
  red-pen" verifiable.
- Any case that fails → excluded from the live-pick dropdown.
- Keep the framing on evidence/procedure, not fiddly statute.
- Pick the 2–3 featured cases (strong → escalate; messy → gap; weak → honest no).

**Thinker:**
- Lock the 3-minute, 3-speaker script (table above): Thinker opens, Engineer
  drives the live beat, Lawyer validates + closes. Each has ~1 min — cut every
  word that isn't load-bearing.
- Run the demo 10× to a stopwatch; 3:00 is a hard ceiling. Record a fallback video.
- Confirm the slot length (3 vs 5 min) with organizers and pick the variant.
