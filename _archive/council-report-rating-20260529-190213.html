✓ What earns the score

  • The thesis is top-tier. "An agent honest enough to tell you that you don't have a case" reverse-engineers the rubric — it is Track B's "recalibration," it is the access-to-justice argument, and it's the most memorable beat any team can run.

▲ What holds it back (the 16 points)

1 · The "no" is unproven — and that's the whole thesis

No calibration set, no false-"no" rate — just "test on a weak case." A wrong "no" (turning away a real claim) on stage in front of solicitors is the worst, and currently unpriced, failure mode. The auditors added: it's not just a demo bug — it's a liability/UPL exposure in front of a regulated host.

2 · The highest-variance demo beat is architecturally undecided

"Judge picks → agent ingests REAL docs live" with heavy email chains and photo folders can stall, blow context, or hallucinate — in front of Anthropic and Google. The brief names the risk but doesn't choose pre-ingest vs live-ingest.

3 · A flagged-but-unresolved contradiction in the demo spine

Damp-only build + "judge picks a provided disrepair case" + "run a weak provided case" — yet only 1 of 10 provided cases is disrepair (and it's social housing). To the brief's credit it flags this and defers to a dedicated cases-council (now answered) — but the brief as written hasn't yet absorbed that fix.

Two quieter risks the auditors raised

The "honest no" is trivially copyable once demoed — a one-day narrative edge, not a moat. And a brief this council-polished risks reading as over-produced to judges who explicitly reward rough working prototypes — your build must look as real as your plan reads.

✎ The five advisor scores in full

99 Expansionist — "highest ceiling in the room" ▸

The rare brief where ceiling is the point, and it finds the highest one in the room. The "honest no / recalibration" framing is a genuine reframe — it converts the judges' verbatim Track-B language into the single most memorable demo beat, and "the most valuable thing it can do is tell you to stand down" is a line judges repeat to each other. The acquisition story is tight: an honest intake brain that lowers cost-to-serve is exactly what Lawhive needs, so the host is also the natural acquirer, with GV/Balderton seeing a horizontal "intake brain for every consumer legal problem" wedge. The stale-AI foil is a kill-shot of timing.

Held off 100 only because the upside is narrated more than demonstrated. Raise the ceiling: (1) one live 10-second "watch it recalibrate a non-disrepair problem" so judges SEE the engine generalize; (2) quantify the stand-down ("saved you ~£X and 9 months") — turning a refusal into visible value.

94 First Principles — "reverse-engineers the rubric almost exactly" ▸

What's actually being judged: a real problem clearly understood, something interactable, an access-to-justice argument that moves the needle, room to grow — explicitly NOT polish. This brief reverse-engineers that rubric almost exactly. The core insight — the highest-value act is an honest "no," demoed on the judges' own cases — is not a feature, it's a thesis about what trustworthy legal AI is. Sequencing PDF-render Day 1, gating 3D, and two lawyer validations on the GTM question show optimizing for interactable-and-defensible over impressive-and-fragile. Top-3 as written.

Why not higher: the whole edifice rests on the agent reliably returning RED on a genuinely ambiguous case live — "wrong no is worse than wrong yes" is asserted, not proven. Fixes: (1) a labelled calibration set with a quantitative Day-8 bar (zero false REDs; AMBER when uncertain); (2) pre-stage a borderline case showing AMBER→route-to-human — converting the worst failure mode into proof the system knows its own limits.

88 Outsider — "the story lands hard" ▸

As a normal person, this story lands hard. The "honest intake brain that talks you OUT of a weak case" is genuinely surprising and instantly trustworthy — it reframes an AI lawyer from "robot that sues everyone" into something on your side, and doubles as a business pitch. The Section-21 angle is the strongest move: ChatGPT confidently citing a law that died this month, beside their tool citing the real source, is a "wow" any judge can feel. The ruthless scoping reads as discipline.

Pause: "honest read on whether you have a case" is a big promise, and a wrong legal call on stage in front of lawyers is the one thing that could sink them. The 3D scan is the only false note — it smells of the "polish" the judges said they don't want. Fixes: (1) cut the 3D scan from the live run entirely; (2) put the housing-lawyer validation FIRST and show "reviewed by a practising housing solicitor" on screen.

87 Executor — "engineered against the failure modes that sink demos" ▸

From the execution lens, this brief is engineered against the exact failure modes that sink hackathon demos, and that discipline is rare. The single best decision is D1: "render a real PDF end-to-end" on day one — output-path risk is the classic hidden time-sink, front-loaded instead of discovered at hour 9. The curated-shortlist-instead-of-text-box call kills the worst live-input variance; the 5/8-min cuts hedge slot uncertainty; the failsafe video + static URL means a stall isn't a faceplant; explicit driver/narrator/failsafe roles matter for a team that's never built together. The "honest no" as Wow 1 is demo-safe — a negative verdict can't be fact-checked into embarrassment the way a fabricated statute can.

What keeps it out of the 90s: the "judge picks → agent ingests REAL docs live" beat is unresolved and the highest-variance moment in the run. Fixes: (1) pre-ingest at selection time into a cached, pre-validated structured summary; (2) stand up the GCP+Gemini path by D11, not D13, behind a Claude fallback.

71 Contrarian — "a load-bearing crack, documented and walked past" ▸

A genuinely sharp plan — the "honest no" wedge maps 1:1 onto Track B's recalibration and the "moves the needle on access to justice" criterion, and letting a judge pick a case live on their files serves "something we can actually interact with."

But a load-bearing crack the team has documented and then walked past: their own §3 admits only 1 of 10 provided cases is disrepair, and it's a council tenancy where the Section-21 hook doesn't map. The demo spine — "judge picks a strong provided disrepair case," "run a weak provided case live" — assumes a supply of provided cases that may not exist. That's a potential demo-killer flagged in their own document and left unresolved. Second crack: the pitch rests on recalibration accuracy, yet there's no calibration target, no confusion-matrix check — just "test on a weak case."

Fixes: (1) build a pre-vetted bank of 3–4 disrepair fact patterns the team controls, so the live demo never depends on the provided-case lottery; (2) add one quantified calibration pass (lawyer-scored, report the false-"no" rate). Do those and this is an 84.

⇄ Peer review — the calibration audit

What the auditors concluded ▸

Best-calibrated score: 4 of 5 auditors named the Contrarian (71) — "the only one that traces its deduction to a falsifiable structural fact (§3's 1-of-10) and shows how it breaks the demo spine." One named the Executor (87) for pricing the live-ingestion mechanism risk most concretely.

Most mis-calibrated: unanimous — the Expansionist (99). "It rewards a narrated ceiling it admits is 'narrated, not demonstrated,' then docks a single point. A 99 on an unbuilt plan is grade inflation — the 28-point gap between it and the Contrarian on the same document exposes the anchoring on rhetoric."

What all five advisors missed:

  • Plan ≠ win probability. "Nobody separated quality-of-the-plan from likelihood-of-winning — they're different scores, and everyone scored a blend, so the 71–99 spread is partly an artifact of undefined units."
  • An unbuilt plan should cap below ~90 on principle — execution is the dominant unproven variable.
  • The "honest no" is trivially copyable once demoed — a narrative edge, not a moat.
  • Wrong-"no" is an asymmetric liability (UPL / safeguarding) in front of a regulated host, not just a demo glitch.
  • Double-counting: calibration + live-ingestion drive nearly every deduction — the real spread is narrower than 71–99 looks.
