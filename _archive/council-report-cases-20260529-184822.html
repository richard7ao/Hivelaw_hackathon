⚖ LLM Council · Verdict #2

Which two cases should anchor the demo?

5 advisors · anonymised peer review · chairman synthesis — run across the 10 provided case files (cases/)

The question put to the council

The revised plan demands a live demo of two real cases from the provided set — one the agent honestly turns away ("talk them down"), one it prepares & escalates. (1) Which case is the best strong→escalate and which the best honest-no→talk-down? (2) Keep the damp-&-mould-only build, or widen — given only 1 of 10 cases is disrepair, and it's a council/social tenancy that breaks the Section-21 "why now" hook? (3) What does the case set reveal that should change the plan?

★ Council Verdict

The whole council agrees on the binding constraint, and it isn't "wow." It's don't let the agent be legally wrong in front of a room of solicitors. Every case pick must survive that test first.

That reframes the choice. The flashiest cases (the dismissal-with-settlement-clock, the lost-appeal) are the ones most likely to make the agent say something a Lawhive solicitor contradicts live — which kills the access-to-justice argument, because a tool that can't be trusted on the law doesn't widen access to anything. Pick cases you can defend cold against a hostile expert.

Recommended · Strong → escalate (hero)
Case 07 — Council damp & mould

It's what you actually built, it's the most human case in the set (single mum, child's cough, GP letter), and it exercises the full stack: prospects → HHSRS → protocol letter + EH complaint → handoff with the dodgy "works completed" form flagged. Reframe the hook to Awaab's Law — which fits this case perfectly and hits harder than Section 21 ever did.

Recommended · Honest no → talk down
Case 04 — Flight delay (ATC defence)

A genuinely defensible "you may not win, and it's £220 — here's the cost/benefit." Light docs (safe to ingest live), the law really is "it depends" so the honest no is correct, not a dodge — and the "no" needs only an assessment, not a drafted letter, so it's cheap to build.

The strong alternative the peer review leaned toward: Case 01 (deposit) → escalate + Case 10 (lost appeal) → talk-down. Choose this if you'd rather put the emotion in the "no" (a retiree talked out of throwing good money after bad) and keep the escalate legally bulletproof (deposit protection is binary, settled law, light docs). But only if your lawyer can make the appeal/CPR reasoning airtight — Case 10's "no" is itself a correctness landmine (set-aside vs appeal, time limits, res judicata). See the decision rule below.

Where the council agrees high confidence

  • Correctness > everything. Unanimous in peer review: the demo's failure mode is the agent misstating law in front of solicitor-judges. This is the lens for every pick.
  • Widen the framing beyond damp-and-mould. 4 of 5 advisors. The product is "honest triage that can say no"; disrepair is the wedge, not the whole product. "Damp-only" optimises the build, not the thesis.
  • Case 10 is the most resonant "no" — a retiree talked out of a hopeless appeal directly answers Lawhive's own Track B question. (But see the landmine below.)
  • The Section-21 hook is broken for these cases. No provided case even involves Section 21 — the one disrepair case is social housing. The hook must change.

Where the council clashes genuine disagreement

1. The escalate case — safe or deep? Advisors split four ways: Case 1 (deposit — clean, safe, forgettable), Case 7 (damp — what you built, most resonant, heavy docs), Case 9 (dismissal — cinematic but legally intricate, a "trap"), Case 2 (photographer — defensible but legally thin). Peer review corrected toward Case 1 on safety grounds. The chairman lands on Case 7 instead — see the recommendation reasoning.

2. The talk-down — resonance vs safety. Case 10 is the emotional gut-punch but legally tricky to get right live; Case 4 is a defensible, low-risk "no" but lower drama. This is the real trade, and it depends on how much you trust your lawyer + rehearsal.

3. Depth vs breadth. "Build a deep engine + 2 templates" (safe, buildable) vs "breadth IS the product — cross-domain intake is the venture story" (ambitious, but unbuildable-in-a-day if taken literally).

Blind spots the council caught in peer review emerged in round 2

  • You have a lawyer — that changes the math. Every advisor treated "intricate case" as fixed risk. With counsel pre-vetting the reasoning and rehearsing it, the harder cases (7, even 9) become defensible. "Too hard to be right live" is partly a false constraint.
  • Case 10's "no" is the hidden correctness landmine. Four advisors parked Case 10 as the "safe" half of the demo — but a dismissed claim with appeal rights is procedurally the trickiest reasoning on the board. The agent explaining why it's dead can itself be wrong live.
  • Widening isn't free. The honest-triage engine is the cheap part; correct domain-specific legal logic for a second deep case is the actual build. Keep the deep build on damp; make the "no" assessment-only.
  • Show the agent routing, not just refusing. "This isn't disrepair — here's the door it belongs to" demonstrates honest intake better than a flat "no."
  • "Potential to go further" has an obvious answer: publish the turn-away rate as a trust metric — the thing no one monetising claims dares show.

→ The Recommendation

Hero = Case 7 (damp). Honest-no = Case 4 (flight delay). Widen the framing, not the deep build.

Why Case 7 over the safer Case 1, against the cautious consensus: (a) it's the domain you spent two weeks building — Case 1 means bolting on a second body of law you didn't prepare; (b) it's far more powerful on the criterion that actually wins this panel — access to justice (a child's health, not a £980 deposit); (c) Awaab's Law turns the "social housing breaks the hook" flaw into a stronger, more current hook — a child died from mould in social housing; new statutory deadlines are phasing in now; the tools to hold landlords to them don't exist. The two objections to Case 7 are both mitigable: pre-ingest the photo bundles (never parse a folder live on stage), and have your lawyer vet the disrepair reasoning cold (it's your prepared domain).

The "no" (Case 4) carries no drafting and settled-enough law that you can't be caught out. Put the emotion in the hero, the integrity in the no.

On scope: reframe the pitch to "an honest front door that can say no — disrepair first." Keep the deep build on damp; add only a shallow assessment path for the no. The "judge picks a case" promise → let them pick from a curated shortlist of 2–3 pre-tested cases, not all 10 (this is the honest reconciliation of interactivity vs. correctness). And retire the Section-21 foil — no demo case touches it; use "AI confidently gets current law wrong" + Awaab's Law instead.

Decision rule (if you don't want to overthink it)

Lawyer confident on disrepair and you can pre-ingest the photo bundles reliably → 7 + 4. Nervous about live document-handling or disrepair scrutiny → fall back to 1 + 4 (safest of all). Want the emotional "no" and your lawyer will nail appeal/CPR law → 1 + 10. Avoid Case 9 as the hero unless your lawyer signs off every line — it's the highest-correctness-risk case in the set.

The one thing to do first

Lock the escalate case now, and pre-ingest its real documents into the engine this week.

The demo's credibility lives or dies on the agent handling the actual provided files without faceplanting — and the heavy bundles (photo folders, long email chains) must be pre-processed, never parsed live on stage. Everything downstream (corpus, letter template, the hook) depends on which escalate case you commit to. Decide it, then make its documents flow start-to-finish before you build anything else.

✎ The five advisors — full responses

Contrarian Pick what you can defend cold against a hostile expert ▸
Escalate: Case 2 · Talk-down: Case 10 · Widen + reframe hook

Picks: Escalate Case 9 is the trap — take Case 2 instead. Talk-down: Case 10.

Here's the fatal flaw nobody's saying out loud: you have ONE disrepair case, it's social housing, and it detonates your entire "why now / Renters' Rights Act" hook on stage. A Lawhive solicitor will catch it in four seconds — Awaab's Law and HHSRS Cat 1 don't touch Section 21, and your pitch will sound like you skimmed a headline. Damp-and-mould-only is a dead end with this case set. Widen scope to "renter/consumer disrepair-of-promise," reframe the hook from "Renters' Rights Act" to "the asymmetry: ordinary people can't price their own claim." That hook survives contact with all 10 cases. The legislative hook does not.

Escalate case: NOT 9. Case 9 (dismissal/settlement clock) is bait — discrimination, victimisation, clawback, covert recordings, a 5-day timer. Build that in a day and the agent WILL state something a real employment solicitor contradicts live. You lose on the one criterion you can't fake in a legal room: being right. Take Case 2 (wedding photographer) — clean breach, force majeure won't save a no-show, distress damages genuinely limited (so the agent demonstrates honest calibration even on a strong case: "you'll win, but not the number you're imagining"). That nuance is your whole thesis, and it's defensible.

Talk-down: Case 10 (lost small claim, wants to appeal). Textbook. Already dismissed, likely out of time, still lacks the expert evidence he lost on — appeals aren't a re-run. The agent says no, explains why, and you've shown the product's soul. Case 4 is the weaker talk-down: "it depends" isn't a turn-away, it's a shrug.

The trap you're walking into: you'll over-engineer the turn-away and under-engineer being correct. The judges said "not judging on polish" — so polish isn't your risk. A solicitor watching the agent confidently misstate the law is. One wrong limitation period, one fumbled "discretionary commission," and the access-to-justice argument dies. Pick the two cases you can defend cold against a hostile expert. That's 2 and 10.

First Principles Ship the judgment, not the niche ▸
Escalate: Case 7 · Talk-down: Case 10 · Engine + 2 deep templates

Strong→escalate: Case 7 (council damp & mould). It is your only disrepair case, it is genuinely strong, and it's the single case where the prepared-document + solicitor-handoff arc is real. Awaab's Law is the live "why now." The social-tenancy/Section-21 break is irrelevant — that hook was for the plan, not the renter. A renter with a coughing child does not care which statute provides the lever.

Honest no→talk down: Case 10 (lost small claim, wants to appeal). Not Case 4. Case 4 is "it depends / low value" — mush, not a no. Case 10 is a clean no: dismissed, directions missed, appeal grounds near-zero, retiree about to spend more. Talking him down is the highest-integrity act in the whole set.

But here's the wrong question. "Damp & mould only" optimizes for your build, not the thesis. The thesis — judges said it outright — is "recalibration": an honest agent that knows the difference between a case and a grievance. If you scope to disrepair-only, your two demo cases come from different domains anyway (7 is disrepair, 10 is small-claims appeal) — so you've already broken the single-domain frame. Don't pretend otherwise.

So don't widen to "all consumer law" (unbuildable in a day) and don't narrow to "damp only" (proves nothing about honesty). Build the honest-triage engine — the recalibration conversation, prospects scoring, the no — as the product, and hard-fund exactly two case templates: 7 and 10. Disrepair is your escalation depth; the appeal is your honesty depth. The engine generalizes; the demos are deep.

What the demo proves: not "we built a damp tool" — every team will build a tool. It proves an agent that says no when no is correct, and that the same judgment escalates correctly when yes is correct. That moves the access-to-justice needle because the 9-in-10 who shouldn't sue currently get told "you might have a claim!" by everyone monetizing them.

Expansionist Demo the honest front door to the entire legal system ▸
Escalate: Case 9 · Talk-down: Case 10 · Widen hard (breadth is the point)

Strong → escalate: Case 9 (dismissal after grievance, settlement clock). Honest no → talk down: Case 10.

Keep damp-and-mould-only? No. Widen — and make breadth the whole point. Disrepair-only is a feature you'd build; cross-domain triage is a company you'd fund. The 10 cases were handed to you spanning deposit, consumer, employment, building, small-claims. That's not noise — that's the judges quietly testing whether you noticed the real problem is intake, not damp. The bottleneck in access to justice isn't drafting one more letter; it's that nobody can afford the first hour that tells them which of six doors they're standing in front of. An agent that reads a messy bundle and says "this is employment, not consumer; here's your prospect; here's your clock" is Case-In-A-Box. That's the venture-scale story, and it's exactly Lawhive's own funnel.

Pick the two cases that prove range, not depth. Case 9 vs Case 10 does that in one breath: same agent, opposite verdicts, two different legal universes. Case 9 is the cinematic one — settlement expiring in 5 days, £4.4k on the table but ~£8k in future bonus and a live victimisation claim, covert recordings, a clawback trap. The agent doing real triage under a countdown clock is the Twitter moment: "It found the deadline the human would've missed." Case 10 is the heart — a retiree, and the agent's most valuable act is talking him out of throwing good money after bad. That directly answers Track B's "what does success look like."

The bigger swing: don't demo a damp letter-writer. Demo the honest front door to the entire legal system — domain-agnostic triage that routes, assesses prospects, and refuses. Six months out: every Lawhive case starts here; the turn-away rate becomes the trust metric nobody else dares publish. Acquire-bait, not feature.

Outsider "Only damp" is a cage — and the one damp case breaks your own banner ▸
Escalate: Case 1 · Talk-down: Case 10 · Widen to renter + consumer

Case picks: Case 1 (deposit) — "prepare it properly and send to a lawyer." A renter's £980 was never put in the legal protection scheme. As a normal person, that's the one that made me go "wow, I'd use that." It's black-and-white: a number, a missing thing, a clear penalty. Anyone can follow it in 60 seconds on stage, and it's a renter, so it's on-brand.

Case 10 (tree-roots, already lost) — "honestly tell them to stop." A retired man already went to court six weeks ago, LOST, and wants to go again with money he doesn't have. The AI saying "please don't — you'll lose again and you can't afford it" feels genuinely kind, like a friend protecting him. Compare that to telling the wedding-photographer couple (Case 2) or the dad with the sick kid (Case 7) "no" — that would feel cruel on stage and make the AI look heartless. Pick the case where "stop" is the loving answer. Case 10 is it.

Now the uncomfortable part. "We only do damp and mould" looks weirdly narrow, and the one damp case undercuts your own pitch. You have ten cases, exactly ONE is damp (Case 7), and that one is a COUNCIL flat — but I'm told your headline is "a private-renting law just changed." So on stage you'd announce a private-renting hook and then demo a council tenant it doesn't cover? A normal person in the audience goes: "wait, does this thing even apply to her?" That's the opposite of the honest-read story you're selling.

Bigger issue: your whole brilliant idea is "honestly tell people if they have a case." That idea works on ANY problem — deposit, car, wedding, wages. Boxing it to damp throws away your best demo (Case 1) AND your kindest "stop" demo (Case 10), neither of which is damp. The narrowness isn't focus; it's a cage. Widen to "renter and consumer problems," demo 1 and 10, and your pitch and your prototype finally tell the same story.

Executor Build the pipe Monday — the law is the easy part ▸
Escalate: Case 1 · Talk-down: Case 4 · Doc-agnostic shallow engine, deep on damp

Picks: escalate Case 1 (deposit not protected). Honest-no Case 4 (flight delay). Not the disrepair case as your hero. Read on.

The hard truth: the spec says damp-&-mould-only, but the demo promise — judge picks from the set, agent ingests REAL docs live — means your engine must survive whatever they hand you. Those two facts collide. You cannot build deep disrepair reasoning AND broad ingestion in a day-plus-prep with a team that's never shipped together. So don't. Build a document-agnostic ingestion + prospects-assessment engine that's shallow but honest, and make damp-&-mould the one case where your reasoning goes deep.

Why 1 and 4 over 7/9/10: Case 1 is 5 clean docs, the law is binary (deposit protected within 30 days — yes/no, screenshots prove no), escalation is a crisp 1–3x penalty claim. Nothing to misread on stage. Case 4 is 5 light docs and the law is "it depends" (extraordinary circumstances), so the honest "you may not have a case" is the correct answer, not a dodge — perfect talk-down. Cases 7/9/10 carry photo folders and zips: live OCR/vision ingestion of a month-1/3/8 mould-photo folder at 4pm on hotel wifi is exactly what faceplants.

Hidden time-sink: document ingestion normalization — PDFs, screenshots, DOCX, image folders into clean text the model can reason over. That's 60% of your build, not the legal logic. What breaks at 4pm: a heavy doc stalls or blows the context window mid-demo, and the judge picked that case.

Fastest path / Monday: Build the ingestion pipeline first (GCP Document AI or just PDF-to-text + vision-to-caption), test it against ALL 10 doc sets so nothing throws live. Hardcode/pre-cache the two hero cases (1 and 4) so they're bulletproof. Let the engine attempt any case the judge picks (broad but shallow), but steer to your two. Ship the talk-down path day one — saying "no case" is your differentiator and the easiest thing to demo. Build the pipe Monday. The law is the easy part.
